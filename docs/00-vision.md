# 00 – Vision

## Elevator pitch

*Everwrit Online* is a World of Warcraft–style MMORPG set in **Vellumar**, a high-fantasy world where the System is part of nature. The gods wrote the world into being. When their divine text shattered, the pieces became **the Chronicle**, a force that levels, titles, and records every living thing. Players are **the Unwritten**: new souls the Chronicle has never recorded, arriving just as **the Redactor** starts erasing the world page by page.

The game plays like a classic MMO and reads like a LitRPG novel. Each session is written up as a chapter of your own story, with stat blocks included.

## What makes it LitRPG (not just an MMO with a UI skin)

| LitRPG trope | How it shows up in the game |
|---|---|
| The System has a voice | The Chronicle comments on your deeds in a dry, ancient voice, through AI-flavored notifications that always have a template fallback. |
| Blue boxes | Level-ups, quests, skill gains, and titles appear in diegetic system windows styled as glowing script on vellum. |
| Stat allocation | 2 free attribute points per level on top of class growth. Respec costs gold. |
| Skills level through use | Every ability has its own **Proficiency** (1–10) and can **evolve** at 10. |
| Class advancement by deeds | **Class Paths** at levels 10/20/30 are offered according to how you played. |
| "World First!" announcements | Server-wide broadcasts, first-kill titles, and the **Hall of Record**. |
| Unique items | **Unique** rarity means one copy per server. |
| Identify / Analyze | **Analyze** is a skill that levels up and shows more of an enemy's stat sheet as it does. |
| Hidden quests and classes | Found through Perception, unusual behavior, or the Scriptborn racial *Marginalia*. |
| The novel itself | The **Chronicle Journal** turns your session log into a LitRPG chapter you can read and share. |

## Design pillars

1. **Your story is written.** Every system feeds the Chronicle. If it happened, it can be narrated.
2. **Growth you can feel.** Short loops (Proficiency ticks), medium loops (levels, free points), and long loops (Paths, evolutions, titles).
3. **Classic MMO feel.** Tab-target combat, the trinity, threat, cast bars, 5-player dungeons, and a 10-player raid. Players should feel at home right away.
4. **Discovery rewards attention.** Some content is only found by players who read, look, and experiment.
5. **Small world, deep systems.** One continent and 30 levels, built to be polished and expanded later.

## Target audience

- **Primary:** MMO veterans (WoW, FFXIV, ESO) who also read LitRPG (*The Primal Hunter*, *Defiance of the Fall*, *Dungeon Crawler Carl*, *He Who Fights With Monsters*, *Awaken Online*).
- **Secondary:** LitRPG readers who have always wanted to play the kind of game the books describe.
- **Session shape:** evening sessions of 1–3 hours. Every session should produce a Journal chapter worth reading.

## Comparables

| Game / book | What we take | What we avoid |
|---|---|---|
| World of Warcraft (Classic era) | Zone flow, the trinity, dungeons and raids, a world that feels social | Two-faction content duplication, huge zone count |
| *The Primal Hunter* / *Defiance of the Fall* | A System that is a force of nature, evolving skills, titles | Power spirals that break balance |
| *Dungeon Crawler Carl* | A System with personality and world-first announcements | Snark as the only tone; our Chronicle is dry, not cruel |
| *Awaken Online* | Class paths tied to behavior | Hidden classes that invalidate normal ones |
| RuneScape | Skills leveling through use | Heavy grinding |

## Scope philosophy (solo / hobby team)

MMOs are the hardest genre to ship. We will ship by staying small on purpose:

- **One continent, five zones, levels 1–30, three dungeons, one 10-player raid.**
- **One player coalition** (the Concord), so every zone serves every player. Players get identity from the five Great Houses, not from two warring factions.
- **One shared humanoid skeleton** for all races, so animations get reused.
- **Stylized, painterly art** built from marketplace kits, which is cheaper and ages better than realism.
- **Every roadmap phase ends playable.** No year-long dark periods.
- **The AI makes the world feel bigger than its content budget** through narration, NPC voices, and journal chapters. It is never needed to play.
